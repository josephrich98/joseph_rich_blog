description: "Radiogenomics proposes to read tumor biology off a CT scan and spare patients a biopsy. Rather than fit another predictor, this study asks how much genomic signal a CT scan could possibly carry: treating the image as a noisy readout of the tumor genome gives a closed-form ceiling — the highest R² any decoder could reach — estimated by canonical correlation on 190 clear-cell renal cell carcinoma patients from TCGA-KIRC with paired contrast-enhanced CT and tumor RNA-seq, and calibrated against a permutation null because the raw statistic inflates when feature count approaches sample size. Across the transcriptome, CT recovered at most 1.7% of expression variance, an a-priori ccRCC gene panel did no better than size-matched random panels, and the strongest apparent signal was patient sex rather than tumor biology (9 of the 10 best-predicted genes were sex-chromosome transcripts; CT decoded sex at AUC 0.81, and demographic and scanner adjustment removed 93% of the signal). The bound on any single genomic score is looser — 0.55 across 38 components, 0.20 within the leading 5 — so a cohort this size cannot exclude a moderate effect for a specific score, but it does argue against using CT to infer genotype or defer molecular testing, and flags that models trained this way risk encoding demographic bias."
